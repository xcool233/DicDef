WORD: Finicky
PRONUNCIATION: ˈfɪnɪki

DEFINITION: Excessively particular or hard to please, especially about small details.
PART_OF_SPEECH: adjective
USAGE: She’s so finicky about her coffee that she weighs the beans before grinding them.

DEFINITION: Requiring great care, precision, or attention to detail; delicate or sensitive.
PART_OF_SPEECH: adjective
USAGE: The old printer is finicky and only works if the paper is loaded exactly right.

SYNONYMS: fussy, fastidious, picky, meticulous, persnickety

ETYMOLOGY: Of uncertain origin, first appearing in American English in the mid-19th century. It may be related to the earlier word “finical,” from Middle English “finical” meaning "over-refined," possibly derived from “fine,” from Old French “fin” (delicate, fine) and ultimately from Latin “finis” (end, limit, boundary).

ADDITIONAL_ETYMOLOGY: The adjective “finicky” is recorded from around the 1840s in the United States, suggesting an American English formation. Linguists often connect it to “finical,” which appeared in Middle English and carried the sense of being excessively particular or affected in refinement.

“Finical” likely developed from the adjective “fine,” which entered English from Old French “fin,” meaning “delicate” or “refined,” itself from Latin “finis,” meaning “end” or “limit.” The semantic shift may reflect the idea of something brought to a fine point or extreme limit of detail.

The suffix “-icky” in “finicky” resembles expressive or diminutive formations in English, possibly influenced by playful or emphatic patterns in American English. Although its exact pathway remains uncertain, the word’s development reflects a blend of Middle English and Old French influence, with deeper roots in Latin.
